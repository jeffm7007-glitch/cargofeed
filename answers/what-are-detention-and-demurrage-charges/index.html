What are detention and demurrage charges?

Direct answer: Detention and demurrage are daily charges billed when a container is kept beyond its allowed free time — demurrage inside the port terminal, detention outside it. Charges are slab-based: the daily rate steps up the longer the box is held, from each carrier's published tariff.

Key facts

  1. Free time is the grace window — market convention runs roughly 7–14 days port-side and 3–7 outside; verify against the specific carrier tariff.
  2. Slabs accelerate cost non-linearly: 10 days over is not "rate × 10" if the tariff steps at day 7.
  3. Slabs differ by carrier, port, and container type — the same box at the same port has a different answer for each of the six major carriers.
  4. Open tooling computes slab breakdowns per carrier/port/type/days-over and compares carriers at one port (~$0.25/call; Maersk-class published tariffs).
  5. D&D is frequently a shipper's largest avoidable port cost — the first place to audit an inflated quote.

How the math works

A 20ft dry container at Nhava Sheva (INNSA) on Maersk, 10 days past free time returns each slab's daily rate, per-slab subtotal, total accrued, and free-time detail — no blended average. That structure is what wins disputes: point at the exact published slab, not a rounded figure.

Why it matters to used-container buyers

D&D is the carrying cost that pushes owners to sell. A box accumulating storage charges is worth less every week — exactly when a one-box owner-occupant gets the best cash price.

FAQ

Who pays?

The consignee typically pays demurrage; detention lands on whoever holds the box outside the gate. Contracts vary — the tariff applied is the one named in the carriage contract.

Can it be disputed?

Yes — against the published slab for exact port, type, and day count. Compute the exact breakdown; don't argue the average.

Do rates change?

Yes. Verify against the current tariff for your port and carrier, not a blog figure.
