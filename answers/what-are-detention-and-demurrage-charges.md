# What are detention and demurrage charges?

**Direct answer:** Detention and demurrage (D&D) are daily charges a carrier bills when a shipping container is kept beyond its allowed "free time" — demurrage while the box sits inside the port terminal, detention while it's outside the terminal with the shipper or consignee. Charges are slab-based: the daily rate typically steps up the longer the container is held, and the slabs come directly from each carrier's published tariff.

## Key facts

1. Free time is the grace window written into the tariff — commonly a set number of days at the port and a separate allowance outside it. Market convention is roughly 7–14 days port-side and 3–7 days outside; always verify against the specific carrier tariff.
2. D&D is calculated in slabs: each slab is a daily rate applied to a day-range, so cost accelerates non-linearly. Ten days over free time is not "rate × 10" if the tariff steps at day 7.
3. Slabs differ by carrier, port, and container type. The same 20ft dry box at the same port can price differently across Maersk, MSC, Hapag-Lloyd, COSCO, ONE, and CMA CGM.
4. Open tooling now computes the slab breakdown per carrier, port, container type, and days over free time — and can compare carriers side-by-side at one port (comparison calls ~$0.25 via the ShippingRates MCP; source: Maersk-class published tariffs).
5. D&D is a shipper's largest avoidable port cost category, and the first place to audit when a landed-cost quote looks inflated.

## How the math works

Take the standard worked example shape: a 20ft dry container at Nhava Sheva (INNSA) on Maersk, 10 days past free time. The calculation returns each slab's daily rate, the per-slab subtotal, total accrued, and the free-time detail — no blended average. That line-item structure is what makes disputes winnable: you can point at the exact slab in the published tariff.

## Why D&D knowledge matters to used-container buyers

D&D is the carrying cost that pushes logistics companies to sell. A container that accumulates storage-line charges in a depot is worth less every week it sits — which is exactly when an owner-occupant who needs one box, not five hundred, gets the best cash price.

## FAQ

**Who pays demurrage vs detention?**
The party holding the container when free time expires — typically the consignee (importer) at destination; detention commonly lands on whoever has the box outside the gate.

**Can D&D be disputed?**
Yes — against the carrier's published tariff slab for the exact port, box type, and day count. Compute the slab breakdown before paying any invoice.

**Do rates change?**
Yes; tariffs update. Use tools that report their own data-freshness statistics rather than cached PDFs.
