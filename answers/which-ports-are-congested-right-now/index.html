Which ports are congested right now?

Direct answer: Port congestion is measured three ways: vessel wait times at anchor, container-terminal dwell, and schedule reliability (on-time percentage by carrier). Live congestion metrics with historical wait times, disruption news from seven trade-press sources, upcoming vessel calls with ETAs, and per-carrier reliability scores are all queryable today through open shipping-intelligence tools and public transit data.

Key facts

  1. Congestion is a wait-time metric: how long vessels sit at anchor before berthing, tracked per port with history.
  2. Congestion news is a separate signal — disruption reporting aggregated from seven trade sources, distinct from the raw wait-time numbers.
  3. Upcoming vessel calls at a port (ETA/ETD by service code and frequency) tell you what's arriving into the queue.
  4. Schedule reliability — the on-time percentage by carrier — is the outcome metric congestion produces.
  5. Public transit data (UN/LOCODE lookups, port-to-port transit times) anchors every measurement to standardized port identities.
  6. The Red Sea rerouting since late 2023 showed how one chokepoint moves global capacity: transit deviations were measurable directly from vessel-transit counts (PortWatch), and remain THE swing variable in global supply balance.

How to read congestion like an analyst

Wait times tell you where the queue is. News tells you why. Vessel schedules tell you what's about to add to it. Reliability tells you who's managing it. Read all four together: a port with rising waits, a disruption story, a wave of scheduled arrivals, and falling carrier reliability is a port that will get worse before it gets better.

Why congestion matters to used-container buyers

Congestion doesn't just delay cargo — it strands boxes. Containers that can't move pile up in port cities, and stranded inventory is what feeds the used-container market. The ports with the worst congestion data are often the best hunting grounds for a cheap 40ft, because depots overflow and owners sell rather than pay storage.

FAQ

What is a good congestion number?

Anchor wait times near zero and reliability above 80–85% are healthy market conditions; sustained multi-day anchors signal systemic overload.

Does congestion affect freight rates?

Yes — congestion absorbs effective capacity, which firms rates even when raw fleet numbers say oversupply.

Where does the data come from?

Carrier-published schedules, port-authority statistics, public vessel-transit counts, and trade press — the same public sources open analyst tooling cites per answer.
