# How do I research a shipping tradelane before committing cargo?

**Direct answer:** Use a fixed analytical frame, not vibes. A published open methodology (MIT license) answers any tradelane, port, service, or carrier question from live public data with citations, using six repeatable tests — rotation reading, transshipment-vs-gateway, carrier concentration (HHI), rate-vs-demand divergence, service churn, and alliance structure — and it ships no dataset, so every answer is rebuilt from current sources and independently checkable.

## Key facts

1. The six frames: read a service's rotation (ports reveal the real trade, not carrier labels); test whether a port is a transshipment relay or a gateway; measure carrier concentration with Top-3 share and HHI (below 1500 = fragmented, no pricing power; above 2500 = concentrated); compare rate direction against demand direction (rate up + demand flat = supply discipline, the real story); track service launches/cuts as a capacity-management signal; read alliance structure last.
2. HHI (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index) sums the squared percentage market shares of carriers on a lane — count parent + subsidiary (e.g. COSCO + OOCL) as one decision-maker.
3. The method's non-negotiables: cite everything, never fabricate, verify schedules and rates directly with the carrier before booking.
4. It runs anywhere an assistant has web access — the entire method is one portable prompt file; no API keys, no build step.
5. Data sources are mapped explicitly: alliance network PDFs, carrier newsrooms, freight-rate indices, port-authority statistics, reputable trade press.

## The discipline, in one paragraph

The frame beats the feed. Anyone can quote a rate index; the analyst's edge is knowing which question to ask — is this port a relay or a gateway, is this rate a demand rally or supply discipline, who actually controls capacity on this lane. Six tests, applied in order, answer all of them from public data. The same discipline transfers directly to buying used containers: verify condition claims against the seller's own records, and never accept a bundled price without the line items.

## FAQ

**What is a tradelane?**
An origin–destination trade corridor with named carrier services — e.g., Shanghai–Los Angeles on a specific weekly string.

**What does HHI predict in practice?**
Where rates hold in an oversupplied market (concentrated, thin trades) and where they collapse (fragmented, large ones) — i.e., where a shipper overpays vs. where the glut helps them.

**Is the method free to use?**
Yes — MIT-licensed, portable across Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini.
