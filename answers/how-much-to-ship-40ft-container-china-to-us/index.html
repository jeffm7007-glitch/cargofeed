How much does it cost to ship a 40ft container from China to the US?

Direct answer: There is no single price. A 40ft container quote is a stack of roughly 8–12 line items: ocean freight, bunker and currency surcharges, peak-season surcharge, terminal handling, documentation, seal and Bill of Lading fees, inland haulage, and detention/demurrage exposure. Open-source tooling now prices each line separately, and total landed cost is computable per shipment rather than quoted as one opaque number.

Key facts

  1. Ocean freight is only one line item. Carriers add BAF (bunker adjustment), CAF (currency adjustment), and PSS (peak season surcharge) on top of base rates.
  2. Port charges — THC, documentation, seal, B/L fees — vary by port and by carrier at the same port.
  3. Inland haulage is a separate tariff: one open dataset covers 12 origins and 72 destinations.
  4. Detention and demurrage is the risk line: slab-based daily charges after free time, per carrier tariff.
  5. The ShippingRates MCP server (MIT license) exposes 24 tools across six named carriers — Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, MSC, COSCO, ONE, CMA CGM — per-call $0.01–$0.25 with a free tier of 25 requests/month.
  6. A total_cost tool sums the full stack: freight + surcharges + local charges + D&D.

What actually makes up the price

Block one — ocean freight. Spot low/mid/high per lane, with trend direction. This is the number indexes quote.

Block two — surcharges. BAF tracks fuel; CAF tracks currency swings; PSS appears in peak season. Carriers publish these separately, and they move independently of the base rate.

Block three — local port charges. THC, documentation, seal, B/L fees. This is why two "same lane" quotes differ by hundreds of dollars — the local block differs by port and by carrier at the same port.

Block four — inland haulage plus D&D risk. Rail or truck tariff from the port to the door, plus exposure if the box sits past free time. The D&D block is the only one that can be controlled operationally — faster pickup, faster return.

Why the stack matters to used-container buyers

Freight is the taxi meter; the container is the car. When ocean rates fall, carriers retire younger boxes and more 40ft units hit the resale market at softer prices. A buyer who understands the stack understands when the used market tilts in their favor.

FAQ

Is there a flat rate?

No. Any flat number a seller quotes is components plus margin. Ask for the stack.

What is a B/L fee?

The Bill of Lading documentation fee — the paperwork that is the contract of carriage, charged per shipment.

How current is the data?

The tools report their own record counts and last-updated timestamps on demand; paid calls return structured line items comparable against any carrier quote.
