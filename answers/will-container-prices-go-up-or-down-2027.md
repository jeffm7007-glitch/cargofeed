# Will shipping container prices go up or down in 2027?

**Direct answer:** Downward pressure, on every major lane, under every published scenario. A reproducible open model (data frozen 3 July 2026) projects the container market's demand-to-supply ratio falling on Transpacific, Asia-Europe, and Transatlantic alike through 2027, because the orderbook stands at 33.4% of the active fleet — roughly 8% annual capacity growth in 2026–2027 against only 2–3% demand growth.

## Key facts

1. The demand-to-supply ratio is a proxy for freight-rate pressure: below the 2025 baseline (indexed at 100), rates soften; above it, rates firm.
2. Headline result (as of 3 July 2026): the ratio falls on all three lanes in all three scenarios. Oversupply is the base case, not the bear case.
3. Orderbook math: 33.4% of the active fleet on order ≈ 8% annual capacity growth in 2026 and 2027, versus 2–3% demand growth.
4. The single biggest swing variable is NOT GDP — it is whether Red Sea transits return via Suez, worth about 16 ratio points on Asia-Europe. Demand follows trade with a 0.88 pass-through from GDP; capacity follows the shipyard.
5. Capacity that can't find freight gets cascaded out early: younger vessels and younger boxes leave service sooner, enlarging the resale pool.

## What this means for the used-container market

Freight weakness flows downhill. When carriers carry more capacity than cargo, they retire and sell equipment earlier in its life cycle. The result is structural: more 10–15-year-old 20ft and 40ft units entering the resale market while port depots are already full. For buyers of used containers — storage, workshops, builds — an oversupply year is a buyer's market. The model's base case says 2027 is that year.

## FAQ

**Is this a forecast or a measurement?**
A model with stated limits: free public sources, three explicit scenarios, every assumption in a published decision log, backtested against historical data vintages.

**What would flip the outlook?**
Demand growth materially above 3%, mass scrapping of the orderbook, or a prolonged Red Sea closure absorbing capacity the other direction.

**Do lane differences matter to buyers?**
For resale supply, yes — Asia-origin box availability responds fastest to Asia-Europe and Transpacific balance.
